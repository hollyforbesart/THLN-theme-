Ferritin and hair shedding: why "normal" may not be the whole story

What ferritin is, why your lab range might not answer your question, and what to ask your provider next.

When my hair started shedding, one of the first clues was a lab value I'd seen hundreds of times as a dietitian and never thought much about: ferritin. Mine was 11.9.

What ferritin is

Ferritin is a protein that stores iron. A ferritin test is often used as an estimate of how much iron your body has in reserve, which is different from how much iron is circulating in your blood at a given moment.

That distinction matters, because a person can have iron stores running low before other markers on a standard blood panel change.

Key takeaway

Ferritin reflects stored iron. It's one piece of information about your body, not a diagnosis on its own.

"Normal" vs. the question you're actually asking

Lab reference ranges tell you how your result compares to a reference population. They don't always answer the question you came in with, which might be: could this be contributing to my hair shedding?

That's a conversation worth having with your provider. A few questions you might bring:

  • What was my ferritin result, and how does it compare to previous results?
  • Were other iron markers or a complete blood count checked alongside it?
  • Is there anything that could be affecting this result, like recent illness or inflammation?
  • If we decide to work on my iron, when should we recheck?

Bring your history with you

Your provider can only work with what they know. Bringing a short summary of when the shedding started, what changed around that time and what you've already tried can make the appointment more useful.

Your hair can tell you that something is happening. Ferritin might be part of it. It might not be the only part.

Ferritin is one clue, not the whole map

In my own story, working on my iron was one piece. The shedding eventually slowed down, but my hair still didn't feel right, and it took a scalp biopsy to learn what else was going on.

That's why it helps to look at the bigger picture. Diet, hormones, stress, illness, medications, your scalp, your styling and your family history can all be part of what's happening.

Good to know

Iron supplements aren't right for everyone, and more isn't automatically better. Talk with your provider before you start one.

What you can do

  1. Get the full picture of your labs. Ask for copies of your results so you can track them over time.
  2. Look at what you're eating. Notice how often iron-containing foods show up in your week.
  3. Write down what's changed. Note when the shedding started and anything that happened in the months before.
  4. Follow up. If you make a change, agree on when you'll check whether it's helping.

When to get help

Some things you can work on yourself. Others belong in the hands of a professional.

Talk to a professional if
  • your shedding is sudden, patchy or getting worse
  • you notice scalp pain, burning, itching or changes in the skin
  • you have other symptoms that don't feel right

Understand what's going on. Do what you can. Get help with the rest.
